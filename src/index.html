Plug a cheap HDMI to USB capture dongle into your laptop and play your PlayStation, Switch, Xbox or retro console right in the browser.

Get the Windows app

Works in Chrome and Edge. Your browser will ask for camera and microphone access, because that's how it sees the dongle.

Three steps, about a minute

Built for playing, not just watching

Low latency

Usually about one frame on top of the dongle itself. Turn on stats to see the number, and low latency mode to push it lower.

Screenshots and instant replay

Grab a screenshot with C, or save the last 30 seconds with V. Screenshots are copied to your clipboard too.

Retro filters

Scanlines and a full CRT look with curved glass and phosphor glow, plus pixel perfect and 4:3 scaling for older consoles.

Picture in picture

Pop the game into a small floating window and keep it on screen while you do something else.

Profiles

Save settings for each console. The right profile loads when its dongle connects.

Private by design

The picture goes from the dongle to your screen and nowhere else. Nothing is uploaded or recorded unless you ask.

Which dongle do I need?

Most capture devices that show up as a webcam work. These are the common ones.

Tested

HDMI to USB 3.0 dongles

Usually built on the MacroSilicon MS2130 chip. Up to 1080p at 60 fps. The best value for gaming.

Works

HDMI to USB 2.0 dongles

Usually the MacroSilicon MS2109 chip. Fine for slower games, but limited to around 1080p at 30 fps.

Works

Capture cards

Devices like the Elgato Cam Link 4K show up as a webcam and work the same way, at 1080p and 60 fps.

Questions

Does it add lag?

A little. The dongle adds its own delay, and the browser usually adds about one frame on top. Turn on stats to see the number on your setup. Fast competitive games feel best on a TV, but most games play great.

Why does it ask for my camera and microphone?

Your computer treats an HDMI dongle as a webcam for the picture and a microphone for the game sound. PortPlay never turns on your laptop's own camera.

Is anything uploaded?

No. Everything happens on your computer. Screenshots and clips are saved to your own Downloads folder.

Do I need the Windows app?

No. The website does everything. The Windows app adds a desktop icon, its own window, and saves screenshots and clips straight to your Pictures and Videos folders.

Does it work on a Mac or Chromebook?

Yes, in Chrome or Edge. The website works anywhere those browsers do.

The picture is choppy. What can I do?

Use a USB 3 port, set your console's video output to 1080p, and pick 1080p and 60 fps in settings. PortPlay also shows tips when it notices a problem.
